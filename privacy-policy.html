2. What we process, and why

Your account

Your e-mail address and password (stored only as a one-way hash), whether your e-mail address is confirmed, the short-lived codes we e-mail you to confirm it, reset your password or change your address (valid for 30 minutes), when your account was created and when it was last active, and your sign-in sessions (each valid for up to 90 days). We use this to create your account, sign you in and keep it secure — performance of our contract with you (Article 6(1)(b) GDPR).

Your training

What you plan and log: workouts and their sessions (dates, times, durations, exercises, sets, weights, repetitions, effort and notes), workout plans, training blocks, your own exercises and preferences about exercises, personal records, goals and weekly targets, and the app’s settings (units, language, appearance, notification choices, training goal, experience, equipment). Optionally, the body measurements you enter — bodyweight, sex, height and age — which the app uses for statistics and strength standards, and the bodyweight it records with each session. Your phone keeps all of it first, and works without a connection; our server keeps a copy, as a backup and to sync it between your devices.

  • We use your training to provide Workouts to you: to store, back up and sync it, and to calculate your statistics — performance of our contract (Article 6(1)(b)).
  • Your body measurements are processed on the basis of your explicit consent (Articles 6(1)(a) and 9(2)(a)), which you give by switching on Use my body measurements — in Settings → Body, or when you first set up the app — and can withdraw at any time by switching it off, which removes them, or by deleting your account. If you entered them before the switch existed, it shows as on. Your training itself we use only to provide Workouts, never to draw conclusions about your health; the app asks for nothing about your health in your notes, and what you write there is kept, synced and shown back to you, and used for nothing else.
  • To sync, we record which of your devices sync, by a random identifier the app creates when it is installed, with its platform and when it last synced — and, on the Free plan, which one is backed up and when you last moved the backup.
  • When you delete something in the app, our server keeps it marked as deleted, so that your other devices delete it too; it is erased with your account.

Apple Watch

If you use Workouts on an Apple Watch, the watch shows your planned workouts and the one in progress, and you log your sets on it. Your training is still written only by the app on your iPhone: the watch sends it what you did — which set, with what weight, repetitions, time or effort, and when — and the app saves it as if you had logged it on the phone, then backs it up and syncs it as described above.

  • Between the watch and your iPhone. The watch exchanges this with the Workouts app on your iPhone directly, over the connection Apple’s system keeps between them; nothing passes through our server on the way.
  • On the watch. The watch keeps the workouts coming up, the one in progress and what it has not yet handed to your iPhone, and a short log of its own technical events, which never contains names, values or your heart rate. It clears them when you sign out of the app on your iPhone or sign in with another account, and when you remove the app from the watch.
  • Heart rate and calories. During a workout the watch measures your heart rate and estimates the calories you burn, and shows them to you. While the watch is connected, the Workouts app on your iPhone shows your heart rate too, and keeps none of it. Neither ever reaches our server, our logs, our usage events or our crash reports.
  • Apple Health. With your permission, the watch saves the workout to Apple Health — strength training, its start and end, your heart rate and active energy — where it counts towards your Activity rings; during the workout it reads your heart rate and active energy there, to show them. On your iPhone, Workouts uses Apple Health only to open the app on your watch when you start a workout on the phone: it reads and writes nothing there. These records are kept on your devices, and by Apple under its own terms, not by us. Beyond what the watch shows you during a workout, we read nothing from them; we never use them for advertising or marketing, we do not sell them, and we store none of them on our server or in iCloud. You can withdraw the permission at any time in Apple Health’s settings, and delete these records in Apple Health.

We process this to provide the watch app you chose to use — performance of our contract (Article 6(1)(b)). What it reveals about your health we process on your explicit consent (Articles 6(1)(a) and 9(2)(a)), which you give by granting Apple Health’s permission when the watch asks for it, at your first workout on it.

The AI Coach

If you use the AI Coach and have agreed to its notice in the app, then each time you ask it something — and once a week, if your plan includes weekly check-ins and you turn them on — we send to our AI provider your message, the conversation so far, the training data the coach reads to answer you (for example your workouts, sets, records, targets, plans and the names and notes you gave them, and strength estimates calculated from your bodyweight and sex), and where you are in the app (the screen, the date, your language and units, your weekly goal, training goal and experience). We do not send your name, your e-mail address or your raw body measurements; each request carries a pseudonymous identifier instead. Your conversations are kept on your account and synced to your devices; with each one, our server also keeps what the coach read to answer you. We use this to give you the coach you asked for — performance of our contract and, for data about your health, your explicit consent, given when you agree to the notice.

For each request we also record its cost and size, and whether it drafted a training programme, without its content. Where the coach is counted in credits, we keep your account’s credits: each grant of them — a month of your subscription, a free trial, a top-up you bought, or credits we gave you, with our reason — when it began and when it expires, what is left of it, whether it was taken back (for example after a refund), and what each request used of it. We use this to keep the coach within your allowance and its costs, and to show you in the app, on its subscription page’s AI Coach card (Settings → Workouts Pro, or Upgrade on the Free plan), what you have left and what used it — performance of our contract.

How the app is used, and when it fails

  • Usage events. The app records events about how it is used — for example that a workout was finished, with its duration and how many exercises and sets it had, or that the plans screen was opened and how it was closed — with your account, a random installation identifier, the app’s version and your platform. If you use an Apple Watch, they also say whether a workout was started and finished on the phone or on the watch, that a watch was connected, and, when a workout from the watch could not be saved as it was, why, from a fixed list of reasons. They never contain your notes, names you typed or your body measurements. We use them to understand and improve Workouts — our legitimate interest (Article 6(1)(f)).
  • Crash reports. When the app fails, it sends us what went wrong: the error and where it happened, the last steps before it (screens opened, requests made, sync events), the app’s version, your platform, system version and device model, the installation identifier and, if you are signed in, your account. On Android, a crash of the app’s native code is reported by Firebase Crashlytics (section 4). When the app on an Apple Watch fails, the watch hands a summary of the failure to your iPhone, which sends it to us like the app’s own crash reports. We use crash reports to fix errors — our legitimate interest.
  • Problem reports. When you send one from Settings → Diagnostics, we receive what you wrote, your device and app versions, the state of sync, a summary of the app’s recent errors and — unless you switch off “Attach recent activity” before sending — a log of the app’s recent activity, which the app shows you first. That log includes the log of an Apple Watch connected to the app, which never contains names you typed, the values you logged or your heart rate. What you wrote, with your device and app details and your account, also reaches our support inbox by e-mail. We use it to answer you, by e-mail to your account’s address, keeping our answer with your report — performance of our contract — and to find and fix the problem — our legitimate interest.
  • Server logs. Our server and the network in front of it record every request: your IP address, the time, the address requested, the answer and how long it took, your device’s user agent and, while you are signed in, your account. We use them to keep the service running and secure — our legitimate interest.

E-mails

When you send us a problem report, we may answer it by e-mail, to your account’s address — also performance of our contract. We do not send marketing e-mail.

Notifications

Reminders — including the one before a free trial ends — the rest timer and the workout in progress on your lock screen are notifications the app shows on your phone, with your phone’s permission; we do not send them from our server. Your notification choices are part of your settings.
